# Failures

Failed leads are retained because they teach the system which apparent patterns are artifacts.

## F-0001: Naive prime digital-root mysticism

Claim: prime digital roots have mystical avoidance behavior.

Result: true avoidance of roots 3, 6, and 9 is explained by divisibility by 3.

Lesson: compare primes to fair null models, not all integers.

## F-0002: Prime gaps have simple base-10 root rhythm

Claim: digital roots of prime gaps reveal a strong cycle.

Result: weak after comparing against modular constraints and shuffled gaps.

Lesson: gap features need nulls preserving parity and small-prime residue constraints.

## F-0003: The triplet pattern `[0, 2, 4]` as a repeatable prime constellation

Claim: three equally spaced odd candidates `n, n+2, n+4` might form a reusable
prime triplet pattern.

Result: obstructed modulo 3. For every `n`, one of `n`, `n+2`, or `n+4` is
divisible by 3, so the only locally prime instance is `3,5,7`.

Lesson: prime-pattern resonance must first pass local residue admissibility.
